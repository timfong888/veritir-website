  <h1>Agentic enrollment, <em>audit first</em></h1>
  <p>Most AI agent pilots in admissions begin by switching an agent on. We think the first thing the software should do is measure whether the office is ready for one, and say which job the agent should take first.</p>
  <dl class="facts">
    <div><dt>Status</dt><dd>Concept<small>seeking design partners</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>Starts with</dt><dd>An audit<small>before any agent runs</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>First pilot</dt><dd>One job<small>one population, capped</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>People</dt><dd>Hold the gates<small>review, approve, take over</small></dd></div>
  </dl>
</div>

<RailSection id="problem" n="01" label="The problem">
  <p class="lead-sm">The work has outgrown the number of people doing it.</p>
  <ol class="principles">
    <li><span class="n">01</span><h3>Too many students per counselor.</h3><p>Southeastern University handles more than 10,000 applications each fall. Its counselors each carried pools of over 1,000 students, and a 24-hour first-contact goal was close to impossible to meet. <a href="#src-seu">[1]</a></p></li>
    <li><span class="n">02</span><h3>Thin, tired teams.</h3><p>In AACRAO's October 2025 survey, 95% of institutions reported at least one admissions staffing challenge, and 54% named burnout. <a href="#src-aacrao">[2]</a> CUPA-HR found 71% of admissions coordinators and counselors had been in the role three years or less. <a href="#src-cupa">[3]</a></p></li>
    <li><span class="n">03</span><h3>A stack nobody designed.</h3><p>One system for records, another for email, a third for texting. Each was a reasonable purchase. Together they cost more, share less, and need outside help to change. <a href="#src-seu">[1]</a></p></li>
    <li><span class="n">04</span><h3>Adoption ahead of readiness.</h3><p>Staff are already using AI tools. Only 56% of institutions in the UPCEA and EducationDynamics survey had a plan to train and support them. <a href="#src-upcea">[4]</a></p></li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="southeastern" n="02" label="One published case" class="prose">
  <p>Southeastern's enrollment team, led by Chief Enrollment Officer Sarah Clark, has described its rollout publicly. What stands out is how narrow the first step was. <a href="#src-seu">[1]</a></p>
  <ul class="plain">
    <li><strong>One population.</strong> Online leads for adult and online programs.</li>
    <li><strong>One job.</strong> A single outreach sequence of six to seven touch points, aimed at getting a prospect to apply.</li>
    <li><strong>A cap.</strong> 1,000 contacts at first, later raised to 10,000.</li>
    <li><strong>People in the loop.</strong> Staff could read every conversation and step in one to one.</li>
    <li><strong>Staff ownership.</strong> Counselors helped choose the agent's name, voice and persona.</li>
    <li><strong>Consolidation.</strong> The move also retired separate CRM, email and texting tools.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The reported results: every lead contacted in the first month, 2% of them converted to applicants, and about $200,000 a year saved on redundant tools. The vendor's case study also reports staff time saved, without describing how it was calculated.</p>
  <p class="aside">These figures come from the vendor's own case study. Veritir has no relationship with Southeastern University or its vendor.</p>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="gap" n="03" label="The gap" class="manifesto">
  <p class="lead">A pilot can only prove what was measured before it began.</p>
  <p>The hard part of an agent pilot is rarely the agent. It is the work around it: knowing today's response times, cleaning the contact data, finding the one current answer to "when is the deadline," deciding when a person takes over, and agreeing in advance what success means.</p>
  <p>Teams with a strong operations lead do this by hand. Most teams skip it, launch, and end up with numbers nobody can defend to a cabinet or a board.</p>
  <p class="turn">So build the audit and the framework <em>into the product</em>, and make them step one.</p>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="audit" n="04" label="Part one: the audit">
  <div class="prose">
    <p>The software connects read-only to the CRM and the public website, then scores seven things. Nothing is sent to a student at this stage.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="prose">
    <p>The audit ends with two things: a baseline that every later result is measured against, and a ranked list of jobs an agent could take, ordered by value and by risk.</p>
  </div>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="framework" n="05" label="Part two: the framework">
  <div class="prose">
    <p>From the audit, the software drafts a pilot charter for the team to edit and sign. An agent cannot be switched on until the charter is complete.</p>
  </div>
  <ol class="questions">
    <li>Which one population, and which one job?</li>
    <li>How many students, at most, in the first month?</li>
    <li>When does a person take over, and who is it?</li>
    <li>What number, measured how, counts as success?</li>
    <li>What result would make us stop?</li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="agents" n="06" label="Part three: the agents">
  <div class="prose">
    <p>Agent jobs ship pre-built, each with its trigger, message plan and human gate already defined. A team picks from the list the audit ranked.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tablewrap">
    <table class="schedule jobs">
      <thead><tr><th scope="col">Job</th><th scope="col">Starts when</th><th scope="col">A person steps in when</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>Inquiry response</td><td>A form is submitted, any hour</td><td>The student asks about cost, aid or fit</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Application completion</td><td>An application sits unfinished</td><td>A document is disputed or unusual</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Visit and event invites</td><td>A prospect matches an event</td><td>A group or special request comes in</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Admit to deposit</td><td>An offer goes out</td><td>The student hesitates or names another school</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Summer melt</td><td>A deposited student goes quiet</td><td>Any sign of a barrier to enrolling</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
  <div class="prose after">
    <p>A scorecard then reports results against the audit baseline in plain units: replies within an hour, applications completed, hours of outreach handled. The method is shown next to every number.</p>
  </div>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="rules" n="07" label="Ground rules">
  <ol class="principles">
    <li><span class="n">01</span><h3>Students know it is AI.</h3><p>Every agent says what it is, and offers a person.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">02</span><h3>Agents do not decide.</h3><p>No admission, aid or scholarship decision is made or implied by an agent.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">03</span><h3>Works with what you have.</h3><p>The audit runs on the current CRM. Replacing tools is a finding, not a prerequisite.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">04</span><h3>Reversible by design.</h3><p>A capped pilot with written stop conditions respects shared governance.</p></li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>
