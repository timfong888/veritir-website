faq:
  - q: "What is agentic enrollment?"
    a: "Agentic enrollment is the use of AI agents that carry out enrollment work, not only answer questions. An agent is given a job, such as replying to new inquiries or following up on unfinished applications, starts on a trigger, works across the CRM and messaging tools within set limits, and hands off to a counselor when a person is needed."
  - q: "How is an AI agent different from an admissions chatbot?"
    a: "A chatbot waits for a question and answers it. An AI agent is assigned an outcome. It starts the conversation, follows up over days or weeks, updates records, and escalates to staff under rules the enrollment team sets in advance."
  - q: "Do AI agents replace admissions counselors?"
    a: "In a sound design, no. Agents take the repetitive outreach: first replies, reminders, status checks and event invitations. Counselors keep the conversations that need judgment, such as cost, aid and fit. Agents should not make or imply admission or aid decisions."
  - q: "Do we need to replace our CRM to use AI agents?"
    a: "Not to get started. The first step is to measure response times, data quality and tool overlap on the systems already in place. Consolidating or replacing tools may turn out to be a finding of that work, but it should not be a prerequisite."
  - q: "What should an enrollment team measure before an AI agent pilot?"
    a: "At minimum: median time to first response, the share of inquiries answered within a day, stage-to-stage conversion by student population, staff hours spent on repetitive outreach, and cost per enrolled student. Without that baseline a pilot cannot show what changed."

  <h1>AI agents for enrollment management: <em>what to get right first</em></h1>
  <p>Agentic enrollment means AI agents that do enrollment work: replying to inquiries, chasing unfinished applications, inviting prospects to visit, and handing off to a counselor at the right moment. Before choosing a platform, a team needs clear answers on four things.</p>
  <dl class="facts">
    <div><dt>Time</dt><dd>How fast<small>does every inquiry get a reply</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>Cost</dt><dd>How much<small>per enrolled student</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>Personalization</dt><dd>How relevant<small>for each population</small></dd></div>
    <div><dt>Integrations</dt><dd>How connected<small>are the systems involved</small></dd></div>
  </dl>

<RailSection id="pressures" n="01" label="The four pressures">
  <h2 class="lead-sm">Four pressures on every enrollment management team</h2>
  <ol class="principles">
    <li><span class="n">01</span><h3>Time.</h3><p>Inquiries arrive at all hours. Counselors work some of them. In AACRAO's October 2025 survey, 95% of institutions reported at least one admissions staffing challenge and 54% named burnout. <a href="#src-aacrao">[1]</a> Slow first replies and missed follow-ups are the visible result.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">02</span><h3>Cost.</h3><p>Each enrolled student costs more to recruit when staff turn over and tools overlap. CUPA-HR found 71% of admissions coordinators and counselors had been in the role three years or less, so teams keep paying to retrain. <a href="#src-cupa">[2]</a></p></li>
    <li><span class="n">03</span><h3>Personalization.</h3><p>First-year, transfer, adult, online and graduate prospects ask different questions on different timelines. One message plan for all of them is cheaper to run and easier to ignore.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">04</span><h3>Integrations.</h3><p>The CRM, student information system, application, email, texting and financial aid tools each hold part of the picture. Institutional knowledge sits in too many places and formats with too little governance, and an agent is only as good as what it can read. <a href="#src-ct">[3]</a></p></li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="up-front" n="02" label="What you need up front">
  <h2 class="lead-sm">What to define before designing an AI enrollment solution</h2>
  <div class="prose">
    <p>Whether you buy a platform or build one, the same inputs decide whether it works. Each pressure has a requirement, and a number you should know before talking to a vendor.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="tablewrap">
    <table class="schedule jobs">
      <thead><tr><th scope="col">Pressure</th><th scope="col">Define up front</th><th scope="col">The number to know</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>Time</td><td>Which outreach is repetitive, and what response standard you are holding yourself to</td><td>Median time to first response; staff hours a week on reminders and status checks</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Cost</td><td>What you spend now on people, tools and outside help to enroll a class</td><td>Cost per enrolled student; annual spend on overlapping tools</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Personalization</td><td>Your populations, and the questions and deadlines that differ for each</td><td>Stage-to-stage conversion for each population</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Integrations</td><td>Every system that holds student contact history, and which one is the record</td><td>Share of records that are duplicated or missing consent to text</td></tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
  <h3 class="sub">Then five decisions, in writing</h3>
  <ol class="questions">
    <li>Which one population, and which one job, comes first?</li>
    <li>Where is the single current answer to each common question?</li>
    <li>When does a person take over, and who is it?</li>
    <li>What number, measured how, counts as success?</li>
    <li>What result would make us stop?</li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>

<RailSection id="funnel" n="04" label="Across the funnel">
  <h2 class="lead-sm">Where AI agents fit in the enrollment funnel</h2>
  <div class="tablewrap">
    <table class="schedule jobs">
      <thead><tr><th scope="col">Agent job</th><th scope="col">Starts when</th><th scope="col">A person steps in when</th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr><td>Inquiry response</td><td>A form is submitted, at any hour</td><td>The student asks about cost, aid or fit</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Application completion</td><td>An application sits unfinished</td><td>A document is disputed or unusual</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Visit and event invitations</td><td>A prospect matches an event</td><td>A group or special request comes in</td></tr>
        <tr><td>Admit to deposit (yield)</td><td>An offer goes out</td><td>The student hesitates or names another school</td></tr>

      </tbody>
    </table>
  </div>
  <div class="prose after">
    <p>Start with one row for one population, with a cap on how many students it reaches. Report results against the baseline in plain units: replies within an hour, applications completed, hours of outreach handled.</p>
  </div>

<RailSection id="rules" n="05" label="Ground rules">
  <h2 class="lead-sm">Ground rules for AI agents in admissions</h2>
  <ol class="principles">
    <li><span class="n">01</span><h3>Students know it is AI.</h3><p>Every agent says what it is, and offers a person.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">02</span><h3>Agents do not decide.</h3><p>No admission, aid or scholarship decision is made or implied by an agent.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">03</span><h3>Works with what you have.</h3><p>Assessment runs on the current CRM. Replacing tools is a finding, not a prerequisite.</p></li>
    <li><span class="n">04</span><h3>Reversible by design.</h3><p>A capped pilot with written stop conditions respects shared governance.</p></li>
  </ol>
</RailSection>
